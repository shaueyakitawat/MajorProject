\begin{abstract}
Derivatives pricing models frequently rely on contemporaneous implied volatility as their central input. This convention induces a circular dependency when the same contract's market premium is utilized both to back-solve and evaluate theoretical option value. This paper presents an end-to-end quantitative framework for detecting and executing model-implied option mispricings in the National Stock Exchange of India (NSE) NIFTY~50 derivatives market without contemporaneous implied-volatility circularity. The pipeline integrates: (i)~an asymmetric EGARCH(1,1,1) model for conditional volatility forecasting; (ii)~a numerical Brent inversion resolving contract-specific implied volatility ($\sigma_{\mathrm{IV}}$) and the Volatility Risk Premium ($\mathrm{VRP}=\sigma_{\mathrm{IV}}-\sigma_{\mathrm{model}}$); (iii)~an econometrically calibrated four-state Gaussian Hidden Markov Model (HMM) classifying latent volatility regimes; (iv)~a redesigned dimensionless composite $M$-Score incorporating price deviation, VRP, and an analytical Gamma risk penalty; and (v)~an automated risk execution desk enforcing dynamic target/stop boundaries, next-day market open execution, and versioned Indian statutory regulatory friction (STT, GST, exchange fees, and brokerage). The architecture is empirically validated through a dual-testing protocol: an out-of-sample walk-forward historical backtest across 346{,}156 positive-volume tradable contracts over 375 continuous trading sessions (July~2024 to December~2025), and forward live paper testing on streaming ticks. The historical backtest generated 2{,}003 closed options trades, achieving a 39.19\% win rate, a 2.16 payoff ratio, a 1.39 profit factor, an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.814 ($R_f=6.50\%$), and a net realized return of +27.39\% (+₹6,84,754.26 on a ₹25.0~Lakh base) while absorbing ₹1,44,654.84 in audited statutory transaction costs. Forward live paper testing across 910 streaming trades independently validates real-time execution feasibility with 14.2\,ms latency and strict delta neutrality ($|\Delta|\le 50.0$), while demonstrating that high-frequency intraday turnover incurs friction drag (-₹93,184.65 net), empirically proving that the econometric edge requires multi-day holding horizons (1.72 days average) to harvest structural volatility risk premia.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{V}{olatility} estimation forms the core of derivatives valuation and risk management. An option contract's market value fundamentally reflects the expected variance of the underlying asset over its remaining lifespan. In classical financial engineering, the Black--Scholes--Merton (BSM) analytical framework \cite{black1973,merton1973} provides the benchmark closed-form solution for European options, but assumes constant diffusion volatility. In reality, asset returns exhibit pronounced volatility clustering, heavy tails, and asymmetric leverage effects \cite{engle1982,bollerslev1986,nelson1991}.

To bypass constant-volatility limitations, market practitioners traditionally invert market prices to obtain Implied Volatility (IV). However, inverting contemporaneous market premiums creates a circular dependency: the market price is used to infer the volatility input, which is then used to claim whether that contract is priced fairly. To establish a genuine statistical edge, theoretical fair value must be derived from an independent, forward-looking econometric forecast rather than the contract's own market quote.

Index options markets, particularly the NIFTY~50 derivatives segment on the National Stock Exchange of India (NSE)---one of the highest-volume derivatives venues globally---exhibit structural dislocations. Equity index options systematically trade at an implied volatility premium over realized volatility, creating a persistent Volatility Risk Premium ($\mathrm{VRP} > 0$) \cite{carr_wu_2009}. While commercial retail trading relies on basic directional technical indicators, institutional quantitative desks exploit structural volatility dislocations by systematically shorting overpriced variance and purchasing underpriced options within rigorous risk boundaries.

This paper develops, implements, and empirically validates an integrated quantitative framework---designated the \textbf{QuantLens Architecture}---for statistical options mispricing detection and automated execution. The study addresses three central research challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item How does out-of-sample econometric volatility forecasting (EGARCH vs.\ GARCH vs.\ rolling benchmarks) translate into independent theoretical option valuation?
    \item How can the Volatility Risk Premium (VRP), non-directional option Greeks, and Hidden Markov Model (HMM) macroeconomic regimes be synthesized into a robust statistical dislocation metric ($M$-Score)?
    \item Does a systematic options mispricing strategy retain positive risk-adjusted alpha after accounting for realistic Indian statutory friction (Securities Transaction Tax, Goods and Services Tax, exchange turnover charges, stamp duty, and brokerage) and portfolio margin constraints?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Key Contributions}
The major contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Decoupled Econometric Valuation Pipeline:} An integrated quantitative architecture that decouples option valuation from contemporaneous implied volatility by combining EGARCH(1,1,1) asymmetric conditional variance forecasts, analytical BSM pricing, and numerical IV inversion.
    \item \textbf{Methodological Differentiation of Performance Layers:} Explicit demarcation across four distinct quantitative dimensions: (i)~\emph{volatility forecasting accuracy} (MAE, RMSE, QLIKE); (ii)~\emph{cross-sectional mispricing detection} ($M$-Score); (iii)~\emph{downstream trading profitability} (net P\&L, Sharpe, Sortino); and (iv)~\emph{execution feasibility} (broker streaming tick DMA, latency, and slippage).
    \item \textbf{Dimensionless Composite $M$-Score Formulation:} A mathematically consistent dislocation metric resolving Greek collinearity, embedding Vega sensitivity via Taylor approximation and penalizing short Gamma risk.
    \item \textbf{Causal 4-State Gaussian HMM Calibration:} Empirical econometric estimation of regime transition probabilities and filtered posteriors, establishing an autonomous volatility regime gatekeeper.
    \item \textbf{Dual Empirical Validation with Audited Indian Statutory Friction:} Full out-of-sample walk-forward historical backtesting (346{,}156 contracts across 375 sessions) with Next-Day Open execution and versioned regulatory friction (pre- and post-October 2024 STT, GST, NSE charges, SEBI fees, stamp duty), paired with 910 live streaming DMA paper executions.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Option Pricing Benchmarks and the Volatility Smile}
Black and Scholes \cite{black1973} and Merton \cite{merton1973} derived the analytical European option valuation formula under geometric Brownian motion with constant volatility. While foundational, the constant-volatility assumption is violated empirically by the presence of the volatility smile and skew across strikes and maturities. Stochastic volatility models (Heston \cite{heston1993}), local volatility surfaces (Dupire \cite{dupire1994}), and parameterizations such as SABR \cite{sabr2002} address the smile. However, these models require daily recalibration to market quotes, preserving the circular dependence on market prices. In this work, BSM is employed as the valuation benchmark, but the volatility parameter is supplied by forward-looking econometric forecasting models.

\subsection{Conditional Heteroskedasticity and EGARCH}
Engle's Autoregressive Conditional Heteroskedasticity (ARCH) \cite{engle1982} and Bollerslev's Generalized ARCH (GARCH) \cite{bollerslev1986} captured volatility persistence and clustering in financial time series. Nelson \cite{nelson1991} formulated the Exponential GARCH (EGARCH) model, which models the natural logarithm of conditional variance:
\begin{equation}
\ln(\sigma_t^2) = \omega + \alpha_1 \left( |z_{t-1}| - \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}} \right) + \gamma_1 z_{t-1} + \beta_1 \ln(\sigma_{t-1}^2)
\end{equation}
EGARCH possesses two decisive advantages: it ensures strictly positive variance without parameter non-negativity constraints, and the coefficient $\gamma_1$ explicitly captures the asymmetric leverage effect (where negative shocks increase volatility more than positive shocks of equal magnitude).

\subsection{The Volatility Risk Premium (VRP)}
The Volatility Risk Premium is defined as the structural spread between implied volatility and realized/forecasted volatility \cite{carr_wu_2009,andersen2007}:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{VRP}_t = \sigma_{\mathrm{IV},t} - \sigma_{\mathrm{forecast},t}
\end{equation}
In equity index options, investors persistently pay a premium for out-of-the-money puts and index straddles as catastrophic market crash insurance. This structural demand drives $\sigma_{\mathrm{IV}} > \sigma_{\mathrm{realized}}$, creating an unhedged volatility risk premium that systematic quantitative strategies can capture through controlled option selling and statistical arbitrage \cite{hull2022}.

\subsection{Hidden Markov Models and Financial Regimes}
Hamilton \cite{hamilton1994} and Rabiner \cite{rabiner1989} developed regime-switching models where an unobserved Markov chain governs macroeconomic state transitions. In financial applications, volatility switches dynamically across low-variance trending environments, normal-volatility expansions, and extreme-panic liquidity contractions. Employing causal filtered probabilities ensures that state classification utilizes only information strictly available at time $t$, avoiding look-ahead bias.

\section{Quantitative Pipeline and Methodology}

The QuantLens framework is structured into eight sequential computational stages (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline_arch}), operating under a dual-source validation paradigm.

\subsection{Dual-Source Data Ingestion Architecture}
To guarantee complete reproducibility and eliminate data snooping, the framework bifurcates data ingestion into two distinct channels:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Historical Empirical Dataset:} Sourced from the official National Stock Exchange of India (NSE) daily Futures \& Options (F\&O) Bhavcopy archives and Yahoo Finance index series (\texttt{\string^NSEI}). The dataset spans 375 consecutive trading sessions from July~1, 2024 to December~31, 2025, containing 605{,}159 total records and 346{,}156 positive-volume, actively traded option contracts across all listed strikes and expiries.
    \item \textbf{Forward Testing Market Feed:} Sourced through an authorized SEBI-registered broker market data API interface providing sub-second REST option-chain snapshots and WebSocket tick streaming during active exchange hours (09:15--15:30 IST).
\end{enumerate}

Consecutive logarithmic daily returns of the NIFTY~50 index are computed as:
\begin{equation}
r_t = \ln\left(\frac{S_t}{S_{t-1}}\right)
\end{equation}
where $S_t$ denotes the official daily closing cash spot price.

\subsection{Econometric Volatility Modeling}
At each session $t$, a rolling historical estimation window of $N_{\mathrm{train}}=60$ trading days is utilized to calibrate the EGARCH(1,1) model with normal innovations $\epsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_t^2)$. The 1-step ahead conditional variance forecast $\hat{\sigma}_{t+1}^2$ is converted to annualized decimal volatility:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\mathrm{model},t+1} = \sqrt{\hat{\sigma}_{t+1}^2 \times 252}
\end{equation}
A dynamic safeguard bounds $\sigma_{\mathrm{model}} \in [0.08, 0.40]$. Econometric benchmarks evaluated alongside EGARCH(1,1,1) include standard rolling 30-day historical standard deviation and symmetric GARCH(1,1).

\subsection{Gaussian HMM Regime Gatekeeper}
Rather than imposing heuristic volatility boundaries, a four-state Gaussian Hidden Markov Model with diagonal covariance is econometrically calibrated on NIFTY~50 daily log-returns and rolling volatility. The transition probability matrix $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 4}$ is estimated via the Baum--Welch expectation-maximization algorithm:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{A} = \begin{pmatrix}
0.9484 & 0.0516 & 0.0000 & 0.0000 \\
0.0493 & 0.9102 & 0.0340 & 0.0066 \\
0.0000 & 0.0949 & 0.9051 & 0.0000 \\
0.0000 & 0.0000 & 1.0000 & 0.0000
\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
States are sorted by conditional annual volatility:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{State 0 (LOW\_VOL):} $\mu_{\mathrm{vol}} = 7.50\%$ ($\sigma_{\mathrm{vol}} = 1.46\%$), persistence $A_{00} = 0.948$, expected regime duration $E[D_0] = 1/(1-A_{00}) = 19.37$ trading days.
    \item \textbf{State 1 (NORMAL\_VOL):} $\mu_{\mathrm{vol}} = 11.45\%$ ($\sigma_{\mathrm{vol}} = 1.49\%$), persistence $A_{11} = 0.910$, $E[D_1] = 11.13$ days. Equilibrium market conditions.
    \item \textbf{State 2 (HIGH\_VOL):} $\mu_{\mathrm{vol}} = 18.82\%$ ($\sigma_{\mathrm{vol}} = 4.21\%$), persistence $A_{22} = 0.905$, $E[D_2] = 10.54$ days. Elevated uncertainty.
    \item \textbf{State 3 (EXTREME\_VOL):} $\mu_{\mathrm{vol}} = 21.21\%$, $\mu_{\mathrm{ret}} = +3.75\%$, $E[D_3] = 1.0$ day. Immediate mean-reversion back to State~2.
\end{itemize}
At each session $t$, regimes are assigned strictly via forward filtered probabilities $\alpha_t(k) = P(S_t = k \mid r_1, \dots, r_t)$ without full-sample look-ahead. In State~3 (EXTREME\_VOL), new short option writing is automatically gated to prevent gamma blowouts.

\subsection{Black--Scholes Theoretical Valuation and IV Inversion}
For each tradable option contract expiring at maturity $T = \max(DTE/365.0, 0.5/365.0)$, fair value $P_{\mathrm{fair}}$ is computed using continuous-dividend Black--Scholes--Merton pricing with Indian benchmark risk-free rate $r=0.065$ (91-day T-bill yield) and dividend yield $q=0.0125$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
d_1 &= \frac{\ln(S/K) + (r - q + \frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\mathrm{model}}^2)T}{\sigma_{\mathrm{model}}\sqrt{T}}, \quad d_2 = d_1 - \sigma_{\mathrm{model}}\sqrt{T} \\
C_{\mathrm{fair}} &= S e^{-q T} \mathcal{N}(d_1) - K e^{-r T} \mathcal{N}(d_2) \\
P_{\mathrm{fair}} &= K e^{-r T} \mathcal{N}(-d_2) - S e^{-q T} \mathcal{N}(-d_1)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Contract Greeks ($\Delta, \Gamma, \Theta, \mathcal{V}$) are evaluated analytically. To determine the market's implied expectations, the observed market closing premium $P_{\mathrm{mkt}}$ is numerically inverted using Brent's method to resolve Implied Volatility $\sigma_{\mathrm{IV}}$:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\mathrm{IV}} = \arg\min_{\sigma} \left| \mathrm{BSM}(S, K, T, r, q, \sigma) - P_{\mathrm{mkt}} \right|
\end{equation}
The contract's Volatility Risk Premium is then resolved as:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{VRP} = \sigma_{\mathrm{IV}} - \sigma_{\mathrm{model}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Dimensionless Composite Mispricing M-Score}
In standard BSM formulations, a first-order Taylor expansion reveals that price dislocation is proportional to Vega and VRP: $P_{\mathrm{mkt}} - P_{\mathrm{fair}} \approx \mathcal{V} \cdot (\sigma_{\mathrm{IV}} - \sigma_{\mathrm{model}}) = \mathcal{V} \cdot \mathrm{VRP}$. Consequently, adding raw Vega to price deviation introduces dimensional collinearity. To eliminate feature overlap and dimensional scaling distortions, the framework formulates a dimensionless standardized dislocation score ($M$-Score) with an analytical Gamma risk penalty:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{dev} &= \frac{P_{\mathrm{mkt}} - P_{\mathrm{fair}}}{P_{\mathrm{fair}}} \\
M\text{-Score} &= w_p \left( \frac{\mathrm{dev}}{\bar{\sigma}_{\mathrm{dev}}} \right) + w_v \left( \frac{\mathrm{VRP}}{\bar{\sigma}_{\mathrm{VRP}}} \right) - w_{\Gamma} \left( \frac{\Gamma}{\bar{\Gamma}} \right) \cdot \mathrm{sgn}(\mathrm{dev})
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\sigma}_{\mathrm{dev}} = 0.05$, $\bar{\sigma}_{\mathrm{VRP}} = 0.02$, $\bar{\Gamma} = 0.0003$, and the calibrated weights are $w_p = 0.50$, $w_v = 0.30$, and $w_{\Gamma} = 0.20$. Because short positions incur negative Gamma risk ($-\Gamma$), the Gamma penalty term explicitly depresses $M$-Score on contracts possessing dangerously high Gamma curvature.

Signal classification is strictly determined as:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{OVERPRICED (SELL Signal):} $M\text{-Score} \ge +1.20$, $\mathrm{dev} \ge +5.0\%$, $\mathrm{VRP} > 0$, and $|P_{\mathrm{mkt}} - P_{\mathrm{fair}}| \ge 2.0$\,pts.
    \item \textbf{UNDERPRICED (BUY Signal):} $M\text{-Score} \le -1.20$, $\mathrm{dev} \le -5.0\%$, $\mathrm{VRP} < +0.02$, and $|P_{\mathrm{mkt}} - P_{\mathrm{fair}}| \ge 2.0$\,pts.
    \item \textbf{FAIR (No Trade):} $|M\text{-Score}| < 1.20$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Execution Management, Margin Governance, and Statutory Friction}
Trading execution is governed by rigorous institutional and regulatory constraints:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Capital Allocation and Dynamic Historical Lot Sizes:}
    The portfolio operates on an initial equity base of ₹25,00,000. Under official NSE circulars across the 375 sessions, NIFTY 50 contract lot sizes evolved as:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \emph{July 1, 2024 to November 19, 2024:} Lot size $Q = 25$ shares (3-lot position = 75 shares).
        \item \emph{November 20, 2024 to October 28, 2025:} Lot size $Q = 75$ shares (SEBI Index Derivatives revision; 1-lot position = 75 shares; 3-lot position = 225 shares).
        \item \emph{October 29, 2025 to December 31, 2025:} Lot size $Q = 65$ shares (3-lot position = 195 shares).
    \end{enumerate}
    Under SEBI peak margin rules, short options require full SPAN plus Exposure margin ($\approx 12.5\% \cdot S \cdot Q$, or ₹2.25--3.85~Lakhs per position). The portfolio restricts exposure to a maximum of 5 short legs and 3 long legs.
    \item \textbf{Next-Day Open Historical Execution Protocol:}
    All predictive modules utilize information $\mathcal{F}_{t}$ strictly bounded at session $t$ close (15:30 IST). Candidate signals are queued for execution and filled at session $t+1$ **Market Open** (`open` price), incorporating half-spread ($0.25$\,pts) and execution slippage ($0.25$\,pts) deductions. If an option does not print an open quote on session $t+1$, the order is cancelled.
    \item \textbf{Market Liquidity Filters:} Candidate contracts must satisfy: (i)~Open Interest $\mathrm{OI} \ge 500$ contracts; (ii)~Daily volume $\ge 50$ contracts; and (iii)~Premium $\ge ₹3.0$.
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Target, Stop, and Gap Boundaries:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \emph{Short Option:} Take-profit at $P_{\mathrm{tp}} = \min(P_{\mathrm{fair}}, P_{\mathrm{entry}} \times 0.75)$; Stop-loss at $P_{\mathrm{sl}} = P_{\mathrm{entry}} \times 1.10$. If session open price gaps above stop-loss, execution occurs at $\max(P_{\mathrm{sl}}, P_{\mathrm{open}}) + \text{Slippage}$. Stop-loss evaluation conservatively takes precedence over profit target if both bounds touch on the same session.
        \item \emph{Long Option:} Take-profit at $P_{\mathrm{tp}} = \max(P_{\mathrm{fair}}, P_{\mathrm{entry}} \times 1.25)$; Stop-loss at $P_{\mathrm{sl}} = P_{\mathrm{entry}} \times 0.90$.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Expiration Settlement:} Unresolved positions held into expiration day settle at intrinsic cash value: $\max(S_T - K, 0)$ for calls, $\max(K - S_T, 0)$ for puts.
    \item \textbf{Historically Versioned Indian Statutory Friction:} Every simulated and live execution deducts:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{Friction} &= 2 \times \mathrm{Brokerage} + \mathrm{STT} + \mathrm{ExchangeFee} \\
    &\quad + \mathrm{GST} + \mathrm{StampDuty} + \mathrm{SEBIFee}
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}
    where Brokerage = ₹20/order, STT = 0.0625\% on sell turnover prior to October~1, 2024 and 0.125\% from October~1, 2024 onwards (Finance Act 2024 revision), Exchange Turnover Fee = 0.053\%, GST = 18\% on (Brokerage + Exchange Fee + SEBI Fee), Stamp Duty = 0.003\% on buy turnover, and SEBI fee = ₹10/Crore.
\end{itemize}

\section{System Architecture and Implementation}

The production framework is implemented in Python and TypeScript across a decoupled institutional architecture:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{High-Performance Backend (FastAPI):} Asynchronous REST and WebSocket engine handling numerical vectorization, EGARCH calibration (\texttt{arch} package), and BSM analytical solvers.
    \item \textbf{Direct Market Access Execution Desk:} Autonomous thread execution manager (`LiveQuantPaperTrader`) executing cycle checks every 5--15 seconds with millisecond tick handling.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Statistical Inference, DSR Analysis, and Reality Check}

The framework's statistical properties were evaluated through four rigorous diagnostic tests:

\subsubsection{Two-Sample Kolmogorov--Smirnov (K-S) Distributional Shift Test}
A two-sample K-S test on normalized trade returns yielded $D = 0.181$ with $p < 0.001$, statistically confirming that the two empirical return distributions differ due to the structural divergence between multi-day holding (1.72 days) and 15-minute intraday tick crossing.

\subsubsection{Welch's Two-Sample t-Test on Horizon Expectancy}
Comparing mean trade P\&L in the daily backtest ($\mu_{\mathrm{backtest}} = +\text{₹}341.86$, $\sigma = \text{₹}6{,}612.59$) and live forward testing ($\mu_{\mathrm{live}} = -\text{₹}99.69$, $\sigma = \text{₹}1{,}313.25$):
\begin{equation}
t = \frac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2}{\sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{n_2}}} = 2.87, \quad p = 0.0042
\end{equation}
The statistically significant difference ($p < 0.01$) proves that high-frequency intraday execution experiences severe friction drag relative to multi-day variance harvesting.

\subsubsection{Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) Analysis}
Following Bailey and L{\'o}pez de Prado \cite{bailey_deflated_sharpe}, the Deflated Sharpe Ratio computes the probability that the estimated Sharpe exceeds zero, explicitly adjusting for sample length ($N=374$ daily returns), positive return skewness ($\hat{\gamma}_3 = +1.792$), excess kurtosis ($\hat{\gamma}_4 = 15.38$), and $K=16$ evaluated configurations:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{DSR} \equiv \Phi\left( \frac{(\widehat{\mathrm{SR}} - \mathrm{SR}^*) \sqrt{N - 1}}{\sqrt{1 - \hat{\gamma}_3 \widehat{\mathrm{SR}} + \frac{\hat{\gamma}_4 - 1}{4}\widehat{\mathrm{SR}}^2}} \right)
\end{equation}
Evaluating against the zero-alpha null ($\mathrm{SR}^*=0$) yields $\mathrm{DSR} = 0.850$ ($z = 1.038$, asymptotic $p = 0.150$). Crucially, while this reflects an 85.0\% probability that the true strategy Sharpe exceeds zero, **it does not achieve conventional statistical significance ($p < 0.05$)**, reflecting the modest 374-session sample length and heavy tails.

\subsubsection{Hansen's SPA, White's Reality Check, and PBO}
Hansen's Test for Superior Predictive Ability (SPA) \cite{hansen_spa} against the benchmark produced $p_{\mathrm{SPA}} = 0.162$. Combinatorially Symmetric Cross-Validation (CSCV) across 8 partitions yielded a Probability of Backtest Overfitting $\mathrm{PBO} = 8.57\%$, indicating low overfitting probability across the 16 parameter configurations.

\section{Discussion}

The findings demonstrate that:
1. **Separation of Forecasting vs.\ Mispricing Detection:** Pure symmetric volatility forecast accuracy (GARCH Normal) does not guarantee trading profitability; asymmetric leverage protection (EGARCH) is required to shield option sellers against volatility spikes.
2. **Horizon Dependency of the Variance Risk Premium:** Options mispricing cannot be captured via intraday scalping; multi-day holding (1.72 days) is essential for theta decay to outpace bid-ask friction.

\section{Limitations}

We explicitly identify three key unresolved limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Daily Bhavcopy Resolution Limit:} The historical dataset lacks intraday order-book depth, precluding modeling of microstructure queue dynamics during market open.
    \item \textbf{Single-Asset Scope:} The architecture is calibrated strictly on NIFTY~50; multi-asset index spreads (e.g., BANKNIFTY) are left for future work.
    \item \textbf{Internal Latency Scope:} The 14.2\,ms latency reflects internal server processing and broker API dispatch, not exchange-matching-engine round-trip colocation latency.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

This paper presented and audited an end-to-end quantitative options terminal framework for the NIFTY~50 market. By decoupling valuation from contemporaneous implied volatility and incorporating a dimensionless $M$-Score with an analytical Gamma risk penalty, the framework bridges econometric theory and execution. Walk-forward testing across 346{,}156 contracts demonstrated robust economic performance (+27.39\% net return, 1.39 profit factor, 0.814 Sharpe ratio) under fully modeled statutory friction, while forward live testing and dedicated ablation experiments diagnosed the critical horizon-dependency of options statistical mispricing.

Future extensions will incorporate multi-asset cross-hedging against BANKNIFTY, calibrate Heston and SABR stochastic volatility surfaces to refine moneyness-dependent skew pricing, and deploy reinforcement learning agents for adaptive intra-day order routing.
